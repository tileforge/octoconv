AT THE CEILING (the run the number comes from)
-----------------------------------------------
  bib-bound, 1 MiB   1 071 659 bytes   373 pages   7 passes    90.0s
  bib-bound, 2 MiB   2 143 444 bytes   745 pages   7 passes   180.3s

Linear in source size, at a constant 7 passes. Both measured, neither
extrapolated.

FULL-PIPELINE CROSS-CHECK, through the live API
------------------------------------------------
The same 2 MiB document, POSTed to /v1/jobs and consumed by the real
cmd/tectonic-worker container:

  wall clock, client's view      190s
  jobs.finished_at - started_at  184.9s
  engine alone (above)           180.3s
  output                         1 914 057 bytes, 745 pages

S3 download + upload + the DB writes cost 4.6s, 2.5% of the job. The engine is
97.5% of it, so a bound on the engine is very nearly a bound on the job.

THE DERIVATION
--------------

  per-pass cost near the ceiling 180.3s / 7 = 25.76 s/pass
  pass ceiling                  7
  margin                        1.5

  TECTONIC_ENGINE_TIMEOUT = ceil(25.76 x 7 x 1.5) = 271s

Every term, stated:

* 25.76 s/pass is measured on a 2 143 444-byte document, not on a smaller one
  scaled up. That document is 2.2% LARGER than the 2 097 152-byte ceiling the
  API enforces, so the per-pass cost covers marginally more work than this
  service will admit -- conservative, and recorded rather than rounded away.
  The discrepancy was found by POSTing the measured file to the live API after
  the ceiling shipped and getting 413 back: "measured at the ceiling" was very
  nearly a claim nobody had checked.

* 7 is the ENGINE's own maximum, not a corpus statistic. Tectonic reruns until
  its references converge and then stops, saying so in its own words:
  "warning: TeX rerun seems needed, but stopping at 6 passes". Every
  bibliography document in the corpus hits it, at every size. This is why the
  derivation is not a p95: with pass counts of 2, 3 and 7 for the same body
  text, a wall-clock percentile would measure the corpus's mix of classes --
  which is a property of the corpus, exactly what .env.example records about
  probe's 600s NOT being derived from its p95.

* 1.5 is probe's margin, and it covers two named things: the 2.5% of pipeline
  outside the engine (measured above), and the difference between this arm64
  host and the deployment. It is not a safety blanket over an unmeasured tail --
  the tail here is bounded by the pass ceiling, which is why 1.5 is enough where
  OCR and pandoc needed 2.0.

* 900s reconciler cap: 271s clears it with a 70% margin.

THE FINDING: A TIMEOUT ALONE BOUNDS NOTHING HERE
--------------------------------------------------
Cost is linear in source size, and this engine inherited the default 100 MiB
per-engine upload ceiling. At 2860 bytes per page (measured on the corpus),
100 MiB of LaTeX is roughly 36 000 pages:

  1 MiB   ->    373 pages ->    90s
  2 MiB   ->    745 pages ->   180s
  100 MiB -> ~36 000 pages -> ~9000s      = 10x the reconciler's 900s cap

So the timeout and the input ceiling are ONE decision, exactly as
PROBE_MAX_PAGES=200 and PROBE_ENGINE_TIMEOUT=600s are one decision. The lever is
the Path-B move OCR already made: bound the INPUT, never shrink the margin to
fit. defaultMaxEngineBytes[EngineTectonic] therefore moves from 100 MiB to
2 MiB, which is 745 pages of dense typeset output -- past any real document, and
measured rather than assumed.

Operator decision, taken 2026-09-04 before any of it was written: 2 MiB / 271s.

THE SHIPPED PAIR, VERIFIED THROUGH THE RUNNING SERVICE
-------------------------------------------------------
POST /v1/inspect on a corpus document, against the API built from this tree
with cmd/tectonic-worker running in compose:

  {"operation":"convert","target":"pdf","engine":"tectonic",
   "estimated_max_seconds":271,"estimate_source":"measured",
   "quota":{"allowed":true},"available":true,"max_bytes":2097152}

Both halves of the decision are on the wire: the measured timeout and the
ceiling it is derived from. estimate_source flipped to "measured" in the same
commit as the number, never before it.

POST /v1/jobs with a 2 143 444-byte .tex:

  {"code":"payload_too_large","error":"file exceeds size limit for this
   conversion type"}

Which is how the 2.2% discrepancy above was found: the document the number was
measured on is, by a small margin, one the service will not accept.
