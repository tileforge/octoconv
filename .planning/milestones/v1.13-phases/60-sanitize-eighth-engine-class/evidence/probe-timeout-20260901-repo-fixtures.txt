THIS RUN DID NOT SET PROBE_ENGINE_TIMEOUT. READ THIS FIRST.

It is committed as the record of what WAS measured, and of what that
measurement is not sufficient for. The corpus is the repository's own test
fixtures: seven files, 331 bytes to 11.6 KB. Production documents are three
orders of magnitude larger, and the phase research had already recorded that
its own 1.9-36 KB measurements were not a basis for a production bound.

Taking this run's recommendation (1s) would have produced a timeout that fires
on the first real document. PROBE_ENGINE_TIMEOUT therefore stays at its 180s
PLACEHOLDER, marked unmeasured everywhere it appears, with an open item at
.planning/todos/pending/2026-09-01-probe-engine-timeout-unmeasured.md.

What this run DOES establish, and what it was worth doing for:

  * the pipeline works end to end against the ghostscript the deployed image
    actually resolves to (10.00.0), not the developer host's 10.07.1;
  * all seven files sanitize AND verify at the deployed cgroup limits
    (2 CPU / 1 GiB), so the measurement harness and the fail-closed corpus
    check are both known to work before anyone points them at real documents;
  * a floor: the pipeline's fixed overhead is ~4 ms for an image and ~100 ms
    for a small PDF, so whatever the real number is, it is dominated by
    document size rather than by constant cost.

recommendation:
  PROBE_ENGINE_TIMEOUT = ceil(p95 x 3) = ceil(0.322 x 3) = 1s

  x3, not pandoc's x2: two ghostscript passes plus a parse give this
  pipeline a longer tail relative to its median than a single-subprocess
  engine has. A probe timeout is classified TRANSIENT (isProbeTerminal),
  so an over-tight bound spends retries rather than failing jobs -- but
  it spends them on every busy minute.

  This is a recommendation, not a decision. The share of timeout-driven
  retries that is acceptable for your load is not something this script
  can know.
