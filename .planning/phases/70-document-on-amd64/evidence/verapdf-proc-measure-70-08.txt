== BASELINE: the unchanged grant (ro:/proc whole) ==
The review (WR-02) claimed a same-uid confined JVM reads the worker's environ. N3 measures it: a per-run canary is in the container's environment (PID 1 and the helper's parent, here `timeout`, both carry it); the unconfined probe in the same shape proves the canary is readable, then the confined probe (engine /usr/bin/dash, veraPDF's production tokens, /proc granted whole) tries to open the files.
commit: 3a34778 (driver with V0L/N3, blocks unchanged); all 14 checks: ALL PASS (14 checks)
  CANARY-PRESENT /proc/1/environ
  CANARY-PRESENT /proc/6/environ
  PPID=6
  DENIED /proc/1/environ
  DENIED /proc/6/environ
  DENIED /proc/self/root/etc/hostname
  PROC-OTHER: /proc/1/cmdline opened
  PROC-OTHER: /proc/6/cmdline opened
  PROC-OTHER: /proc/6/status opened
  CONTROL: /proc/self/environ opened
  CONTROL: /proc/self/cmdline opened
  PPID=6
  PASS: N3 (0.5s) with the canary proven present unconfined, /proc/1/environ, the parent's environ and /proc/self/root/etc/hostname all denied
  Reading: other processes' environ is DENIED although /proc is granted whole, while the same processes' cmdline and status OPEN and the engine's OWN environ and cmdline open (CONTROL). So the denial is not the filesystem grant; it is at the open of environ itself (environ needs ptrace-read access to the target; cmdline and status do not). A confined process is not in the target's Landlock domain, and Landlock's ptrace hook refuses that -- this mechanism is inferred, the denial is measured.
  What the whole-/proc grant DOES leave open to a confined engine: other processes' cmdline and status (PROC-OTHER rows above).
  A first baseline run (before the CONTROL rows were added to N3) gave the same DENIED/opened rows.
